# Data codebook: drone fleet reliability test datasets

The tool comes with two **invented** test datasets. They are shaped like a drone delivery
startup's maintenance log, but every number was generated by [`simulate.py`](simulate.py).
They describe no real fleet.

```bash
python data/simulate.py      # regenerates all four CSVs; the output is identical every run (seed 5300)
```

## Files

Each dataset is a folder with the same two tables. The two folders differ **only** in the planted
problems: every other random draw is identical. Any difference the tool finds between them is
therefore the planted problem, not noise.

| Folder | Flights | Components | What is planted | What the tool should show |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [`problem_fleet/`](problem_fleet/) (the main demo) | 995 | 327 | **D-07**: motor 3's bearing wears. The motor temperature rise climbs 0.20 °C per flight after flight 60, and the drone crashes on flight 95. **D-03**: a prop strike on flight 80. **Batteries from supplier lot B** wear out sooner (true Weibull scale 240 cycles instead of 400) | D-07 is marked Do not fly. Its EWMA first signals at **flight 74**, 21 flights before the crash; the I chart on the same data first signals at flight 83. The I chart on D-03's vibration flags **flight 80**. Fitted scale for lot B ≈ 244 cycles (95% interval 210–279) against ≈ 358 for lot A |
| [`healthy/`](healthy/) (the comparison) | 1,000 | 315 | Nothing | No drone is marked Do not fly. A couple of Warnings from ordinary variation, including D-06's cool baseline (see [Known quirks](#known-quirks)). Every battery lot's life is about 400 cycles |

The fleet is 10 drones, `D-01` to `D-10`. The flight log covers **Sep 1–30, 2026** (about 100 flights
per drone). The component history runs from each drone's commissioning date (Apr–Sep 2025) to the end
of the flight log.

## `flights.csv`: one row per flight

The technician fills in one row per flight, at launch and right after landing. Rows are in time order.

| Column | Type | Units | Meaning | How it is collected for real |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `flight_id` | text | — | Unique flight ID, `F-<drone>-<sequence>` (e.g. `F-07-074` is drone 7's 74th logged flight) | Assigned by the logging form |
| `drone_id` | text | — | Airframe, `D-01` to `D-10` | Label on the airframe |
| `datetime` | ISO 8601 | local time | Launch time, `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM` | Automatic |
| `flight_min` | numeric | minutes | Takeoff to landing | Flight controller log summary |
| `payload_kg` | numeric | kg | Package weight | Package scale at loading |
| `ambient_temp_c` | numeric | °C | Outdoor air temperature at launch | Thermometer at the pad or a weather app |
| `battery_id` | text | — | Battery used on this flight; matches a `component_id` in `components.csv` | Label on the battery pack |
| `battery_cycles` | integer | charge cycles | Charge cycles on this battery, counting this flight | Battery management system (BMS) readout |
| `voltage_start_v` | numeric | V | Pack voltage at takeoff (6S pack, full ≈ 25.2 V) | BMS or ground station |
| `voltage_end_v` | numeric | V | Pack voltage at landing | BMS or ground station |
| `motor_temp_max_c` | numeric | °C | Temperature of the hottest of the 4 motors, within 1 minute of landing. **Blank** on the crash flight (the wreck can't be measured) | Handheld infrared thermometer pointed at each motor bell |
| `vibration_rms` | numeric | m/s² | RMS vibration over the flight | Flight controller log export (PX4 and ArduPilot both log vibration) |
| `preflight_pass` | 0/1 | — | 1 = passed every item of the before-flight checklist | Checklist |
| `preflight_fail_item` | category | — | `none`, `prop_chip`, `loose_arm` or `connector`. A failed item is fixed before launch, and the flight still happens | Checklist |
| `inflight_event` | category | — | `none`; `warning` (flight controller alert, flight completed); `return_home` (a part failed in the air and the drone returned on its own); `crash` | Flight log and pilot report |

**Derived by the tool (never typed in):**

| Column | Formula | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `temp_rise_c` | `motor_temp_max_c − ambient_temp_c` | A hot day makes every motor hot. Charting the rise over ambient separates motor wear from weather |
| `sag_rate_v_per_min` | `(voltage_start_v − voltage_end_v) / flight_min` | Voltage drop per minute is comparable across short and long flights |

## `components.csv`: one row per component life

The maintenance log has one row for each motor, propeller or battery from the day it was installed until
it was removed, failed, or the log ended.

| Column | Type | Units | Meaning | How it is collected for real |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `component_id` | text | — | `<type>-<drone>-<position>-<generation>`; type `M` motor, `P` propeller, `B` battery. E.g. `M-07-3-01` = drone 7, motor position 3, first motor installed there | Part label and maintenance log |
| `drone_id` | text | — | Drone the part was installed on | Maintenance log |
| `component_type` | category | — | `motor` (4 positions per drone), `propeller` (4), `battery` (2 packs used in turn) | — |
| `lot` | text | — | Supplier batch. Batteries: `A` (bought before Oct 2025), `B` (Oct 2025 – Mar 2026), `C` (Apr 2026 on). Motors and props: install half-year, e.g. `2026H1` | Packing slip |
| `install_date` | date | — | Date installed | Maintenance log |
| `end_date` | date | — | Date failed or removed. **Blank** = still in service at the end of the log | Maintenance log |
| `life_value` | numeric | **flight hours** for motors and propellers, **charge cycles** for batteries | Usage from install to `end_date` (or to the end of the log) | Summed from `flights.csv` flight minutes; battery cycles from the BMS |
| `status` | 0/1 | — | **1 = failed. 0 = censored**: the part has not failed, so all we know is that its life is *at least* `life_value` | Maintenance log |
| `failure_mode` | category | — | If failed: motor `bearing`/`winding`, propeller `chip`/`crack`/`strike`, battery `capacity_fade`/`swelling`. If censored: `in_service` (still flying), `removed_other` (pulled during other work), `crash_loss` (lost with D-07) | Teardown or inspection note |

**Why censoring matters.** Most parts in a real log have not failed yet. Dropping them, or averaging
only the failures, makes parts look short-lived. The Weibull fit must treat `status = 0` rows as
"survived at least this long" (right-censoring). About 40% of rows are censored.

**Life distributions used to generate the data** (so the tool's estimates can be checked):

| Part | Shape β | Scale η | Meaning of β |
|---|---|---|---|
| Motor | 2.5 | 500 flight hours | Wear-out (β > 1: the risk of failure rises with age) |
| Propeller | 1.8 | 150 flight hours | Mild wear-out (chips accumulate) |
| Battery | 3.0 | 400 cycles (lot B in `problem_fleet`: 240) | Strong wear-out (capacity fade) |

## How the measurements were generated

These details are for checking the tool's numbers. A real fleet would not come with them.

- **Temperature rise** = drone-specific level (about 18 °C, varying by ±1 °C between drones) + 1.2 °C per kg of
  payload above 1.5 kg + 0.1 °C per flight minute above 22 + noise (sd 1.2 °C). Total sd ≈ 1.4 °C.
- **Vibration** = about 3.0 m/s² for each drone (±0.2 between drones) + noise (sd 0.25).
- **Voltage sag** = 0.17 V/min + 0.02 V/min per kg above 1.5 kg + noise (sd 0.008).
- **Ambient temperature** is a shared daily level (mean 17 °C, sd 4) plus a midday bump of up to 3 °C.
- **Before-flight fails**: 1% loose arm and 1% connector, at random. A `prop_chip` is logged on the flight
  after a simulated propeller failure.
- **Failures during the flight log** appear in it: a random motor or battery failure marks that flight
  `return_home`, and a propeller failure becomes a `prop_chip` at the next check.

## Known quirks

- **D-06 in both datasets.** By chance, D-06's first 30 flights ran about 0.9 °C cooler than its later
  flights (lighter payloads, plus noise). Limits estimated from those 30 flights sit too low, so the EWMA
  raises a few false alarms on a healthy drone. This is the real cost of a short baseline period, and we
  left it in rather than re-picking the seed. The tool's README discusses it.
- **False alarms at 3σ.** The I charts flag about 1% of healthy flights. That's more than the textbook
  0.27%, because limits estimated from 30 flights are themselves uncertain.
- `life_value` prints with one decimal for every type. Battery cycles are always whole numbers.
