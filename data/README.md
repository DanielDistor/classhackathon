# Data codebook: the simulated drone fleets

**Two test datasets**, from the same simulator with different seeds:

| Folder | Name | Seed | Special causes | Warned and replaced (B10) | Failed in flight |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `fleet/` | **Fleet A** (main demo) | 5300 | 17 | 99 (84 at age, 15 caught worn) | 0 |
| `fleet_b/` | **Fleet B** | 7100 | 11 | 119 (104 at age, 15 caught worn) | 0 |

Same design and files; independent part lifetimes, problems and timing, so Fleet B is a second, independent
check of the tool (all its causes caught, the tool matches the technician on all 30 charts, the Weibull fit
recovers the true lives). The dashboard's dropdown switches between them. **The rest of this codebook
describes Fleet A**; every column and rule applies to Fleet B unchanged.

The tool comes with **invented** test fleets. It is shaped like a drone delivery startup's maintenance
records, but every number was generated by [`simulate.py`](simulate.py). It describes no real fleet.

```bash
python data/simulate.py      # rewrites data/fleet/ (7 runs, about 15 s); identical output every time (seed 5300)
```

**Retirement runs.** Parts retire at **B_p**, the age by which p% of that part type fails. The fleet is
simulated once per p = 1, 2, 5, 10, 15, 20, 30% into `fleet/runs/pNN/` (same seed, so the same part
lifetimes and special causes; only the retire rule differs), with `fleet/runs/summary.csv` comparing them.
`fleet/*.csv` is the **B10** run, and every number below is for B10. The dashboard's slider switches runs.

| Retire at | Motor / prop / battery replace-at | Warned and replaced | of which caught worn | Failed in flight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | 113 h / 14 h / 105 cycles | 312 | 9 | 0 |
| B5 | 191 h / 33 h / 163 cycles | 140 | 10 | 0 |

Retiring later saves parts but lets more of them reach visible wear; the charts caught every one in time.

## What is simulated

**10 drones (`D-01` to `D-10`) × 300 flights each, Jul 1 – Sep 29, 2026 (about 3.3 flights a day).**
Nothing is planted on a named drone or flight. Every signal in the data comes from one of two kinds of
assignable cause, both random:

1. **Wear (natural causes).** Every part has its own Weibull life. In the last stretch of that life (its
   **P-F interval**: the time between the wear becoming detectable, P, and functional failure, F) the
   part pushes one reading upward in a straight ramp, reaching +4 sd at failure:

   | Part | Slots | Weibull β | Weibull η | P-F interval | Reading it pushes up |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | Motor | 4 | 2.5 | 500 flight hours | 25 h (~68 flights) | Motor temperature rise (the hottest motor is what's measured) |
   | Propeller | 4 | 1.8 | 150 flight hours | 8 h (~22 flights) | Vibration (the 4 props add up) |
   | Battery | 2 packs, used in turn | 3.0 | 400 charge cycles | 30 cycles (~60 flights) | Voltage sag per minute, on that pack's flights |

2. **Special causes.** One physical cause per Nelson / Western Electric rule shape, each placed at a
   random drone, flight and part (1 + Poisson(1) of each across the fleet, all after flight 60, so the
   50-flight baseline stays clean, and at least 40 flights apart on the same drone and chart):

   | Rule shape | Cause | What it does to the reading | Fix |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | 1: one point beyond 3σ | Prop strike | Vibration +6 sd on the strike flight, then +1 sd from the chipped prop | Replace the prop |
   | 2: 9 in a row on one side | Hard landing | Bent motor shaft: temperature rise +1.5 sd, sustained | Replace the motor |
   | 3: 6 in a row trending | (the wear above) | Ramp over the P-F interval | Replace the worn part |
   | 4: 14 alternating | Bad battery cell | +2.5 sd sag on that pack's flights only; packs alternate, so readings zig-zag | Replace the battery |
   | 5: 2 of 3 beyond 2σ | Loose power connector | +3 sd sag on a random 40% of flights | Repair |
   | 6: 4 of 5 beyond 1σ | Prop imbalance after a field repair | Vibration +1.3 sd, sustained | Replace the prop |
   | 7: 15 within 1σ | Vibration log over-filtered by a firmware update | Vibration noise shrinks to 0.2×: readings too flat | Repair (revert setting) |
   | 8: 8 beyond 1σ, both sides | Loose motor mount | Vibration +1.8 or −1.8 sd each flight (two rattle modes) | Repair (tighten) |

   The rule shape is the pattern each cause is built to produce. The rule that fires *first* is often
   another one: in this run the EWMA or rules 1 and 5 got there first for 12 of the 17 causes. Every cause
   was detected within 0–12 flights (median 1).

### The technician in the loop

Before the flight log starts, the startup ran every part until it broke. The shop fits a Weibull to that
history and adopts each type's **B10** (the age by which 10% have failed) as its replace-at age. In this
run: motor 240.3 h, propeller 47.9 h, battery 197.2 cycles (true values 203.3, 43.0 and 188.9). At the
log start, anything already past its B10 is replaced. These replace-at ages are written to
`replace_at.csv`, and the dashboard schedules with them.

After every flight a simulated technician runs the tool's own statistics (`app/qc.py`) and acts:

| Trigger | Action | `maintenance.csv` reason |
|---|---|---|
| A part fails in flight (the drone returns home: `inflight_event = return_home`) | Replace before the next flight. **None in this run** | `failed in flight` |
| During a drone's first 50 flights (its baseline): a reading is more than 3 sd from the **fleet's normal range** (`fleet_reference.csv`) | Same as a chart signal below | reason ends `(fleet rule 1)` |
| A chart signals (EWMA, or any of the 8 rules on the I chart, after the 50-flight baseline) | A special cause found is fixed before the next flight. A part found inside its P-F interval is **warned and replaced before the next flight**. Nothing found = a false alarm: logged as `no_fault`, nothing is changed | `special cause: …`, `worn: found after a chart signal`, `chart signal, nothing found: a false alarm` |
| A part reaches its B10 | **Warned and replaced before the next flight** | `reached replace-at age (B10)` |

A warning always means "replace before the next flight", so every planned replacement is warned on the
flight it is replaced after.

**The fleet check closes the baseline blind spot.** A drone's own chart can only start once its first 50
flights have set its limits. Before that, each reading is compared with the fleet's normal range: the mean
and standard deviation of 200 acceptance flights (20 per drone, all-new parts, flown before the log). Only
rule 1 is used, because that range includes the differences between drones, and pattern rules or the EWMA
would flag drones that are simply a little different. In this run it caught three wearing propellers
(D-05 at flight 17, D-09 at 39, D-06 at 43) that would otherwise have failed in flight, and raised no false
alarm. Without it, two props failed in flight during the baseline.

After every logged action on a chart (replacement of a part that drives it, repair, or false alarm), its
EWMA restarts at the center line and its run rules start counting afresh. The control limits are kept,
because a new part puts the drone back on its baseline. **No drone ever stops flying.** The tool does the
same from the CSVs (`qc.monitor_chart`, reading the resets from `maintenance.csv`), and the tests check
that it signals on exactly the flights where the simulated technician acted: all 30 drone charts agree.

## Files in `fleet/`

### `flights.csv`: one row per flight (3,000 rows)

| Column | Type | Units | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `flight_id` | text | — | `F-<drone>-<sequence>`, e.g. `F-07-074` is drone 7's 74th flight |
| `drone_id` | text | — | `D-01` to `D-10` |
| `datetime` | ISO 8601 | local time | Launch time, `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM` |
| `flight_min` | numeric | minutes | Takeoff to landing |
| `ambient_temp_c` | numeric | °C | Outdoor air temperature at launch |
| `voltage_start_v` | numeric | V | Pack voltage at takeoff (6S, full ≈ 25.2 V) |
| `voltage_end_v` | numeric | V | Pack voltage at landing |
| `motor_temp_max_c` | numeric | °C | Hottest of the 4 motors, within a minute of landing |
| `vibration_rms` | numeric | m/s² | RMS vibration over the flight (flight controller log) |
| `inflight_event` | category | — | `none`, or `return_home` (a part failed in the air; it is replaced before the next flight) |

Derived by the tool, never typed in: `temp_rise_c = motor_temp_max_c − ambient_temp_c` and
`sag_rate_v_per_min = (voltage_start_v − voltage_end_v) / flight_min`.

### `components.csv`: one row per part life (431 rows), the Weibull input

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `component_id` | `<M\|P\|B>-<drone>-<position>-<generation>`, e.g. `P-03-2-05` = drone 3, prop position 2, fifth prop there |
| `drone_id`, `component_type` | Where it flew and what it is (`motor`, `propeller`, `battery`) |
| `lot` | Supplier batch = install half-year, e.g. `2026H1` |
| `install_date`, `end_date` | `end_date` **blank** = still in service at the end of the log |
| `life_value` | Usage from install to `end_date` (or the log end): **flight hours** for motors and props, **charge cycles** for batteries |
| `status` | **1 = failed** (or a potential failure, see below). **0 = censored**: all we know is that it lasted at least `life_value` |
| `failure_mode` | Failed: `bearing`/`winding`, `chip`/`crack`, `capacity_fade`/`swelling`, or `worn_potential_failure`. Censored: `in_service`, `replaced_scheduled` (reached B10), `removed_other` (history: pulled during other work), `damaged_<cause>` (removed because of a special cause) |

Two censoring choices, both standard and both checked:

- **A part found worn is a potential failure (`status = 1`).** It was inside its P-F interval, so it was about
  to fail. Counting it as censored would hide most early failures and bias the Weibull shape upward. Over
  20 seeds, 95% intervals covered the true values 17–20 times out of 20 when worn parts count as failures,
  but only 14 out of 20 for propellers when they count as censored. The price is a slightly low scale η,
  because the potential-failure age comes a little before the true failure.
- **A part damaged by a special cause is censored.** External damage is a competing risk, not wear-out.

Fitted to this file (lifelines, right-censored maximum likelihood, 95% intervals): motor β 2.62 [1.66,
3.58], η 508 [392, 623]; propeller β 1.91 [1.66, 2.15], η 154 [139, 169]; battery β 3.57 [2.64, 4.50],
η 356 [317, 396]. Every true value is inside its Bonferroni-adjusted interval (the test); the battery η is
just outside its plain 95% interval, which happens about once in 20 by construction.

### `maintenance.csv`: one row per technician action (255 rows)

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `drone_id`, `flight_id`, `datetime` | The action happened after this flight, before the next |
| `action` | `replace`, `repair` (no part changed) or `no_fault` (a chart signalled and nothing was wrong: a false alarm; the chart restarts, nothing else changes) |
| `chart` | The reading the action concerns (`temp_rise_c`, `vibration_rms`, `sag_rate_v_per_min`): the chart that restarts after it |
| `component_type`, `position`, `removed_id`, `installed_id` | The part swapped (blank for repairs and false alarms) |
| `warned_after_flight` | The flight the part was warned after: the same flight it was replaced after (blank if it failed or was damaged without a warning) |
| `reason` | Why, from the trigger table above. Chart-driven reasons end with what fired, e.g. `(EWMA; rule 1)` |

### `replace_at.csv`: the shop's replace-at ages (3 rows)

One row per part type: `component_type`, `unit`, `b10` (the replace-at age), the Weibull `beta` and `eta`
it came from, `n` and `n_failed`, and `fitted_on` (the parts history before 2026-07-01). The dashboard
schedules replacements with these, so no part shows as overdue. Refitting on all of `components.csv` gives
lower motor and battery ages (215 h and 190 cycles, closer to the true 203 h and 189 cycles); the dashboard
shows that refit next to the schedule.

### `fleet_reference.csv`: the fleet's normal range (3 rows)

One row per reading: `chart`, `center`, `sigma`, `n` (200 acceptance flights) and `source`. Used only for each
drone's first 50 flights, rule 1 only (see above).

### `answer_key.csv`: the injected special causes (17 rows)

The simulation's answer key. **The tool never reads it**; the tests use it to check that every cause was
caught. Columns: `event_id`, `drone_id`, `rule_shape`, `cause`, `chart`, `component_type`, `position`,
`start_flight`, `detected_after_flight`, `detected_by`, `fixed_after_flight`, `fix`, `how`.

## How the readings are generated

For checking the tool's numbers. A real fleet would not come with these.

- **Temperature rise** = drone level (about 18 °C, ±1 between drones) + 1.2 °C per kg of payload above 1.5 kg
  + 0.1 °C per flight minute above 22 + noise (sd 1.2) + the hottest motor's wear or damage. Total
  in-control sd ≈ 1.45 °C.
- **Vibration** = drone level (about 3.0 m/s², ±0.2) + noise (sd 0.25) + all 4 props' wear + special causes.
- **Voltage sag** = 0.17 V/min + 0.02 V/min per kg above 1.5 kg + noise (sd 0.008) + the flying pack's wear
  + special causes. Total in-control sd ≈ 0.014 V/min.
- **Ambient** = a shared daily level (mean 17 °C, sd 4) + a midday bump of up to 3 °C + noise.
- Readings are rounded as logged (0.1 °C, 0.01 V, 0.01 m/s²), and the simulated technician charts the
  rounded values, so its decisions match what the tool computes from the CSVs.

## Known quirks

- **False alarms are the price of using all 8 rules.** For a stable process, rules 1–8 together trip
  roughly once every 50–90 points, against once in 370 for rule 1 alone, and the EWMA adds a little more.
  Limits estimated from a 50-flight baseline add a little more. In this run 139 chart signals (1.9% of the
  7,500 readings after the baselines) were false alarms, none during the fleet check.
- **Some baselines are unlucky.** D-05 collects 29 of the 139 false alarms, mostly on motor temperature:
  by chance its first 50 temperature readings were steadier than its later ones, so those limits sit a little
  tight. D-05's first 17 flights also carry the wear of the propeller the fleet check caught, which is
  inside its vibration baseline. We kept the seed rather than re-pick it.
- **Same-minute flights.** Take-off times are drawn independently, so 12 pairs of one drone's flights share a
  logged minute (and some overlap). The tool orders flights by time, then flight number, so this cannot
  reorder a chart; a real logbook would not have it.
- `life_value` prints with one decimal for motors and props; battery cycles are whole numbers.
